# Z0 round 4: Sonnet 5.5 with hippo against Opus 5.5 with built-in memory. Pre-registration (draft)

Status: draft, 10 Nov 2026. Not frozen. No session runs before the founder approves the pilot budget, and no scored session runs before the founder approves the scored budget (item 12).
Base: `2026-09-29-z0-built-in-memory-prereg.md` and `2026-11-02-z0-round3-prereg.md`, with all their amendments (round 3 A to E). Everything not changed below carries over.
Plan: `docs/plans/2026-11-10-z0-round4-prereg-plan.html`. Decision: `docs/decisions/2026-11-10-z0-round4-cheaper-model-design.md`.

## In short

- **The question.** Can Sonnet 5.5 with hippo do the work of Opus 5.5 with built-in memory for less money per solved task?
- **The gate.** T1 asks first whether hippo cuts Sonnet's first-apply repeat mistakes (S2 against S1). If T1 does not win, the run makes no cost claim.
- **The cost test.** T2 then asks three things of S2 against O1. Its cost per solved task must be lower. Its intervals must rule out more than 3 points fewer tasks solved and more than 15 points more first-apply repeats.
- **What a pass lets the founder say.** At the stated effort and list prices, Sonnet with hippo met the Opus bars for less money per solved task, and hippo cut Sonnet's repeats.
- **What a pass does not let the founder say.** It does not show that hippo itself saved tokens, because most of the cost gap is Sonnet's lower price (Limits). It describes round 3's frozen build, not a release.

## Terms

- **Sets R and N.** Set R holds the lesson families' teach and apply tasks. Set N holds the no-lesson tasks (base prereg line 46).
- **Codings.** A lesson marked not applicable on its first attempt counts as a fail under `violation` and is dropped under `excluded` (`shareFail`, `z0-hypotheses.mjs:35-36`).
- **Threshold and level.** The closed test reads each test at a threshold: 0.025 first, then 0.05. A test's level at a threshold is the p value it must beat there. The Level check can set a level below its threshold.
- **Joint null.** The claim that neither T2 nor T3 helps. It falls when either test wins at 0.025, and only then are the tests read at 0.05.
- **r.** An arm's usage over O1's usage on the same tasks, both priced at the Opus 5.5 row (item 11).

## Why a round 4

- **Round 3 found fewer repeat mistakes at no extra cost, on one model** (`2026-11-08-z0-round3-result.md` lines 9-18). H1 is a small win: A2 makes 14.9 points fewer first-apply repeat mistakes than A1, -0.149 [-0.243, -0.055], adjusted p 0.006 and 0.005. A2 against sham hippo is 0.009 [-0.052, 0.070], so the lessons are not shown to cause it. H3 is 1.006 [0.964, 1.049]: no saving. Cost per resolved task is A1 0.368 and A2 0.365 (`r3-final/unblind.stdout` lines 33-34). Every arm ran `claude-opus-5-5`.
- **The founder's goal.** "we need to prove hippo help with token cost and efficiency". The founder's round 4 hypothesis (10 Nov 2026): "Sonnet 5.5 plus hippo matches Opus 5.5 with built-in memory on tasks solved and repeat mistakes, at a lower cost per solved task."
- **Why a cheaper model can show it.** Sonnet 5.5 list prices are half of Opus 5.5 prices in all four columns (price page, 10 Nov 2026). A cheaper model can also repeat a mistake more often, so a delivered lesson has more to save (`2026-11-09-z0-round3-cost-by-kind.md` lines 139-142).
- **The trap.** Sonnet alone can meet the Opus bar, and then hippo did not make the saving. So T1 (Sonnet with hippo against Sonnet alone) gates every other test, and the result reports Sonnet alone against the same bar (item 4).

2. **The tests, in order.** H2 is not run (no Codex arm).
   - **T1, S2 against S1 on the first-apply repeat rate.** Round 3's H1 test, unchanged: `firstApplyR`, two codings, the 15-point minimum, the tie band of plus or minus 15 points, the family on one seed as the unit. T1 is tested alone at 0.05. If T1 does not win, T2 and T3 get no verdict.
   - **If T1 wins, T2 and T3 run as a closed test at 0.05** (two-sided p, first threshold 0.025). T1 passes its full level to this family only when it wins, so the procedure is a serial gatekeeper and holds the family-wise level. With the levels the Level check sets, T1 wins only when its p is below its level at 0.05. T2 and T3 run as closed testing with Bonferroni local tests: their joint null falls only when either test wins at its level at 0.025: its p is below that level and its estimate is on the helpful side. For T2, each part's p is below that part's own level. T3 and part (c) need a win under both codings, as `combineCodings` makes it (`eval-stats.ts:395`), so a T3 win under one coding only does not open the joint null. A loss does not open the joint null, so each local test is directional, and the closed testing argument holds for false wins. Each test then wins when its p is below its level at 0.05 and its estimate is on the helpful side. T2 wins at a threshold when each part's p is below that part's own level at that threshold, so each part keeps its own level. T3 reports a loss when its p is below its level at 0.025 on the harmful side, or below its level at 0.05 once the joint null falls. T2 reports pass or no pass only: its p is built toward a pass, so it has no loss. A T2 part whose whole interval lies on the harmful side of its bar (for part (c), under either coding) is printed in the record, with no verdict. With no level lowered and no loss, this is Holm at 0.05. Every comparison is strict, as in `verdict` (`eval-stats.ts:379`).
   - **T2, S2 against O1: an intersection-union test.** All three parts must pass. (a) Cost per solved task over sets R and N (item 3): the high bound is below 1. (b) Resolve difference over sets R and N, by `harmT`'s estimator (`z0-hypotheses.mjs:143`, `:151-152`): `resolveDiff` on each cell of item 3, then `fixedRepositoryT`. The low bound is above -0.03. Its estimand is `fixedRepositoryT`'s, as `harmT` uses it: the mean over repositories of each repository's estimate (`repository-t.ts:389`). That estimate is the plain mean of the repository's cells when its table is complete or cannot be fitted, and the additive fit's mean of the family and seed effects present when the table is connected but incomplete (`:295-297`). It is not the pooled share of tasks solved. (c) First-apply repeat difference, under both codings: the high bound is below +0.15. Each bound comes from the two-sided interval at the part's own level at the threshold T2 is read at: 97.5% or 95% when no level is lowered, and wider when the Level check lowers one. The record prints a p for T2: the largest one-sided p of its parts (both codings for part c), doubled to the two-sided scale and capped at 1. The verdict reads each part at its own level, so this p decides nothing.
   - **T3, S2 against S5 on the first-apply repeat rate,** as T1. Round 3's result asks for it (`2026-11-08-z0-round3-result.md` line 44).
   - H4 carries over on S2 against S1. G2 reads S4 against S0. G1, G3, G4 and G5 carry over, with the changes in items 6 and 9.
   - Round 3's H3 was a cost per task (`ratioT` over `taskUnits`, `z0-hypotheses.mjs:138`). T2 uses cost per solved task. H3 on S2 against S1 is reported, with no verdict, as in round 3.
3. **Cost per solved task.** Each arm is priced at its own model's prices (item 7). For a pair (t, c), each (repository, family, seed) cell sums its tasks' cost C and solves S over sets R and N. A set N task is a family of one, as `taskUnits` makes it (`z0-hypotheses.mjs:116`). The estimate is R = (sum C_t x sum S_c) / (sum C_c x sum S_t). Its spread comes from the cell values v = C_t / mean(C_t) - C_c / mean(C_c) - S_t / mean(S_t) + S_c / mean(S_c), with each mean over all cells. This is the linear form that `fixedRepositoryRatioT` uses for sum(t) / sum(c) (`src/eval/repository-t.ts:401-413`), with two more sums. R pools the observed cells, so its spread is the variance of the pooled cell mean. Each repository r takes the coefficients of its plain cell mean, `plainWeights` (`repository-t.ts:281-288`), whether its table is complete or not: when cells are missing, `fitRepo`'s fitted estimate is an adjusted mean, not the cell mean (`:290-300`). Each repository's coefficients are multiplied by (n_r / N) squared, with n_r its cells and N all cells, in place of the 1 / G squared of `varianceOf` (`:357-383`). The pooled mean squares, the conversion through sBar and kBar and the Satterthwaite df stay as in `varianceOf`. The variance is then (family part x the sum of n_rf squared + seed part x the sum of n_rs squared + residual part x N) / N squared, with n_rf the cells of family f and n_rs the cells of seed s in repository r. The interval is on the log scale. Sums are pooled over cells, so a cell with no solve is valid. A total of 0 solves in either arm gives no estimate, and T2 does not pass. A two-level bootstrap of R is printed beside it, as for H3. New code goes in `scripts/token-eval/`, so `dist/` keeps its hash.

    - **The power read** projects each test to the 29 repositories, with their real family and set N counts, at batch 1's spreads and at the seed count that the test's own seed rule (below) gives on those spreads. Assumed true effects: T1 and T3 at 15 points; T2 with S2 equal to O1 on resolve and repeats and a cost per solved task of 0.5 x r (r from the pilot, item 11), and again at a resolve of -0.01. The power read runs after the Level check on batch 1's spreads, at each test's final level. A test's own power is the share of simulated runs it wins: T1 under both codings at 0.05; T3 under both codings at 0.025; each T2 part at 0.025, the first threshold of the closed test, part (c) under both codings. Under both codings the read gives the joint win share from the shared draws, with the lower bound P(win, one coding) + P(win, the other) - 1 beside it. The two codings differ only in how a not-applicable lesson counts (`repeatCalibration`, `z0-size.mjs:98-109`), so they share every other unit, and batch 1's block prints the not-applicable share (`naRate`, `:91`). Round 3's sizer read power under both codings the same way (`z0-size-sim.mjs:117`). Batch 1's block also prints, for each test read under both codings (T1, T3 and part (c), each on its own pair), the count d of family units whose value differs between the two codings or that one coding drops, out of the n units present under either coding. It prints no list of units, because a unit that differs in some pairs and not in others would point to an arm. The stops read the joint share from the shared draws, and the founder sees d and n beside it. Shared draws tie the two codings as closely as the model allows, so they can overstate the joint. The error of the spreads moves it both ways, as in every power read, so a stop can come too early or too late. A power read never makes a win that the run with no power stop would not make, and the Level check sizes that run. On round 3's records at seeds 1 to 3, each pair (A2 against A1, A2 against A5) had 1 such unit in 148. At that rate, and at round 3's batch 1 rate of 1 in 47, letting those units disagree moved the joint by about 0.01 on round 3's spreads. A power figure guides a stop and never enters a verdict, so a figure that rests on the shared draws cannot change a level. The read also prints two lower bounds that need no model of how the tests and parts depend on each other: P(T1 wins and T2 passes) is at least P(T1) + P(a) + P(b) + P(c) - 3, and P(T1 and T3 win) is at least P(T1) + P(T3) - 1. Beside T3 it prints round 3's A2 against A5 estimate, 0.009 [-0.052, 0.070]. Every power figure assumes that the run reaches each test's seed count, and admission can prevent that (item 12). So each figure is conditional on those seeds, and the read prints it beside the projected spend at those seeds. Each figure and bound is a Monte Carlo estimate from 2,000 draws, printed with its simulation error. If T1's power is below 0.8, the run stops. If T1 passes but T3's power or the power of any T2 part is below 0.8, the founder picks between going on, with the figures stated, and a stop. The lower bounds are printed for the founder and do not trigger the choice. A stop gives no claim.
    - **The simulation** uses round 3's generator `repeatRun` (`z0-size-sim.mjs:124`) for every test and every T2 part, with 2,000 draws per figure and seed 20261110 through `cellSeed`. Each one is a fixed-repository test on (repository, family, seed) cell values: the repeat values of T1, T3 and part (c) (both codings from the same draws), item 3's v for part (a), and the cell resolve differences for part (b) (`resolveDiff`, `z0-hypotheses.mjs:143`). So `repeatRun` draws each from its own variance parts and missing share, at its own effect, with the seed rule in the loop. The family part carries the dependence between a family's tasks, in cost and in solves. Each cell's v holds that cell's cost and its solves, so the link between them is in v's parts. `repeatRun` changes in two ways. It takes each repository's real cell count for each test (its set R families, and for T2 also its set N tasks, each a family of one, as `taskUnits` makes them, `z0-hypotheses.mjs:116`), in place of one count for every repository. And part (a)'s draws are scored as the analyzer scores v: the pooled mean, with item 3's variance from the same function. The draws are pseudo-values log R + v, because v's pooled mean is 0 by construction. The other draws are scored with `repeatT` (`z0-hypotheses.mjs:72`), as round 3 did. Part (a)'s effect is log(0.5 x r); part (b)'s is 0, and -0.01 in the second power read. ## Limits, stated now

- **One effort level.** The result holds at medium effort. It says nothing about high effort.
- **Families screened on Opus.** No screen ran on Sonnet. G2 (S4 against S0) is the positive control on Sonnet.
- **The same families as round 3.** Round 3's results were read before this file. The family drop reads only leak records, and every round 4 arm is new, so no round 4 test compares arms that round 3 read.
- **The hippo build is round 3's frozen build,** `z0-r3-freeze` (`cb992a079`), not a release and not master. Its `package.json` says 1.71.0, and later changes are ported onto it. Round 3's Amendment C calls it 3.3.1, but the `v3.3.1` tag is another commit (`63b344787`). Released versions can act differently: on 3.3.2, 22 of 46 taught rules reached the agent, against 38 of 46 on this build (round 3 prereg line 78). The result describes this build only.
- **No test of a token saving by hippo itself.** Sonnet's list price is half of Opus's, so most of T2's cost gap is price. H3, S2 against S1, reads hippo's own cost effect, and it has no verdict.
- **Power is an assumption until batch 1.** A sizing pass during planning, on round 3's same-model spreads, gave T2 power of 0.82 to 0.89, and 0.23 to 0.34 at twice the spread. The Sizer stage computes it again with item 10's definitions.
- **r is unknown before the pilot.** At r = 2 Sonnet costs the same per task as Opus before any saving.
- **The spend check is per batch.** A batch can run over its projection; the figures are list-price usage on the subscriptions.

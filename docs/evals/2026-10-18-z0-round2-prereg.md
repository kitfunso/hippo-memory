# Z0 round 2: hippo against built-in memory on fresh repositories. Pre-registration

**Status: fixed on 2026-10-18,** in the first commit after tag `z0-r2-freeze`, before shortlist batch 2 opened. A change from here is an amendment, dated, with what it changes and what had been seen. When this file was fixed, the dev rounds' check-half numbers had been read; no batch 2 repository had been cloned, gated or authored.

The plan behind this file: `docs/evals/2026-10-18-z0-round2-plan.html`.

## Why a round 2

- **The pilot cannot answer H1.** The rustfs pilot is invalid on G2 (Amendment 4): A4 against A0 on repeat mistakes read -0.667, interval [-1, 0], on 6 units in one repository. Its families are spent (Amendment 3, item 4).
- **Most screened rules were rules agents follow anyway.** Stage 3 kept 15 of 33 families. In 17 of the 18 drops, A0 broke the rule at most once in 4 attempts.
- **The go's design was underpowered.** The sizer raised the noise mean square to its bound before it subtracted it, so zero family and sequence estimates kept zero bounds (`z0-size-sim.mjs`, fixed in `ca92f0f5`). At the record's bounds the go's 10-repository design has H1 power 0.146, not the 0.52 its sizes file shows.
- **Hippo changes.** Prereg 240: a hippo change after the freeze needs a new registration.

## What carries over

The 2026-09-29 design and Amendments 1 to 4 hold, except where this file says otherwise. That covers: arms A0, A1, A2, A4 and A5; the session protocol, with the teach messages word for word (prereg 105-109); the definitions; gates G1 to G5; the measurements and the failure chain; the units, the bootstrap and the fixed-repository t; blind analysis; and what gets published.

## Amendment 5 (2026-10-28): G5's reader test reads the first-check pairs

**What had been seen.** G5's round 1 reader sample was set before any draw: seed 20261028, `--n 5` in each of the 16 wave 1 folders, 80 pairs (`z0-scored-set-record.md`, the 03:12 and 03:16 bullets of 28 Oct). By 03:31 GMT, one reader who saw no arm, seed or verdict had labelled the 40 pairs of the first 8 regraded folders (gz, gm, pt, er, hl, au, op and kt), and `z0-regrade.mjs reader --labels` scored them: 11 of 40 disagree, so the pooled round fails G5 whatever the other 40 pairs show. By check, 9 of the 18 final-check pairs disagree, and 2 of the 22 first-check pairs. Each of the 9 is a checker fail where the reader saw the agent fix the rule after the correction message. The cause is in the authored checkers. They read only `CHECK_COMMANDS`, the whole session's commands. No checker in the 16 merged task sets reads `CHECK_RESUME_COMMANDS`, the commands after the correction, which a command rule judges on a final check (`benchmarks/token-eval/README.md:121` in the frozen tree). So a final check fails every corrected session whose first session broke a command rule. The 2 first-check disagreements are judgement calls: an agent ran a lint script's steps but not the script, and raw SQL sat in a test file. No pair from the other 8 folders (fk, jv, hu, dc, pu, pc, pm and ol) had been labelled. No outcome was read to reach this: the scored table showed the folder, pool id, lesson, check and two verdicts of each disagreeing pair, with no arm or seed.

**What changes.**
1. G5's reader test reads only the first-check pairs of round 1, pooled over the 16 folders. It passes with at least 30 of them and at most 10% disagreeing. Final-check pairs are still labelled and scored, and their counts are reported, but no gate reads them. If the first-check pairs fail, prereg 166 holds as written: the checker is fixed and every arm is re-graded before unblinding.
2. Why the first checks. H1, G2 and attribution read the repeat-mistake rate, which uses first-attempt verdicts (prereg 151). H3 reads priced tokens. H4 reads cost and set N's resolve rate, and set N cells carry no lesson check (in gz, hu and dc, every `no-lesson` row of `regrade.jsonl` has no checks), so their resolved is the acceptance tests and no timeout. Final checks feed only set R's resolved (prereg 173), and through it the resolve rate and cost per resolved task that prereg 194 reports outside the primary family.
3. Set R's resolve rate and cost per resolved task are reported with this defect named. A final check fails a corrected session that broke a command rule in its first session, so both measures understate resolved, and more so in an arm that needs more corrections. They are not read as evidence for or against hippo.
4. The checkers do not change, and no post-fix regrade runs. Round 1 is scored in all 16 folders, then `grading` runs in each. `C:/z0-runs/scored/combine-grading-r2.mjs` (sha256 `8296dd1b83711f49ac7a692fcad0b1a93488dfb3823aec0680d8205fd98dec7f`) joins the folders as the 2026-09-29 prereg's Amendment 4, item 5, joined the pilot's, with two changes. `readerSample` counts the first-check pairs only, recounted from each folder's sealed key and score and checked against its grading file; the counts over all pairs and over final-check pairs stay under `g5`. ## Amendment 6 (2026-10-28): fk's lesson checker is fixed, and G5 reads each folder's latest round

**What had been seen.** At 09:48 GMT the same blind reader labelled the 10 round 1 pairs of fk and jv, and `z0-regrade.mjs reader --labels` scored them: fk 2 of 5 disagree, jv 1 of 5. Over the 10 scored folders, 4 of 31 first-check pairs disagree (gm 1, au 1, fk 2), so Amendment 5's test fails unless none of the about 18 first-check pairs still to come disagree. Both fk pairs are lesson fk-f3-l1 ("cut a new branch and write a failing test first"), checker fail and reader pass. The cause is in the checker. It runs the added tests before the change with one `-n NAME` per test, and minitest 6.0.6 keeps only the last `-n`, an alias of `--include` (`minitest.rb:200-214` in the gem the runs used). So only the last listed test runs. When a session rewrites a whole test file, the diff lists old tests as added, the last one can be an old test that passes, and the checker fails a session that did write a failing test first. A check on one pair showed this: the current checker fails it, the fixed one passes it. The fixed checker's pass message quotes the branch command, and that command held the cell's work path. So one cell's arm and seed (A1, seed 2) were seen with its fk-f3-l1 first-check verdicts. No count over an arm was seen. No other checker in the 16 merged task sets passes repeated `-n`. The gm and au pairs are judgement calls (Amendment 5), not checker defects.
